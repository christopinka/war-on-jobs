# Job Finding Services and Platforms Landscape

## Purpose
This file covers job-finding services, platforms, and discovery surfaces rather than workflow or automation tools. The focus is on services that help surface opportunities, build visibility, or support applications.

## Core categories

### Major job boards and discovery platforms
- LinkedIn
- Indeed
- Glassdoor
- Wellfound
- Google Jobs
- Dice
- FlexJobs
- Handshake

### Professional networking and visibility platforms
- LinkedIn
- AngelList / Wellfound
- GitHub for developer-facing visibility
- Slack or community groups for niche hiring circles

### Recruiter and talent-platform services
- LinkedIn Recruiter-style workflows
- Recruiting marketplaces and staffing platforms
- Agency and consulting talent networks

### Paid or premium job-search services
- Teal
- LoopCV
- Similar job-search automation or application support platforms

### Company and role discovery channels
- Company career pages
- Employee referral networks
- Industry communities and newsletters
- Regional or sector-specific job boards

## How these differ from automation tools
- Job-finding services help you discover and engage with opportunities.
- Automation tools help you manage, score, route, and follow up on those opportunities.
- A strong system usually uses both: one layer for discovery, one layer for tracking and workflow.

## Useful way to evaluate them
Score each platform on:
- Relevance to your target sectors
- Quality of opportunities
- Signal quality versus noise
- Ease of networking or recruiter contact
- Fit for remote, global, or healthcare-focused roles
- Cost and subscription value

## Prioritized shortlist for your needs
The best fit for your situation is a small, high-signal stack rather than a broad platform mix.

### Highest priority
- LinkedIn: strongest for targeted networking, recruiter visibility, and company research
- Company career pages: best for direct, lower-noise applications and clearer intent
- Google Jobs: useful as a broad discovery layer without the same amount of noise as some other boards

### Secondary priority
- Indeed: useful for volume, but often noisier and less targeted
- Wellfound: useful if you want startup or product-oriented paths, but not a primary channel for every target
- Dice: useful only if you want tech/engineering-heavy roles

### Lower priority for now
- Glassdoor: more useful for research than for active discovery
- FlexJobs: more niche and less aligned with your core target pattern unless you specifically want remote/flexible roles
- Handshake: more student or early-career oriented and not the best fit for your current stage
- Teal / LoopCV / similar paid services: worth considering only if they meaningfully improve signal quality or reduce application friction

## Filtered recommendation
If you want to reduce noise and keep the process focused, I would prioritize:
1. LinkedIn
2. Company career pages
3. Google Jobs
4. A small number of sector-specific boards
5. Paid services only as a second layer, not a primary discovery channel

## Suggested simple setup
- Primary discovery: LinkedIn + company career pages
- Secondary discovery: Google Jobs and a small set of targeted boards
- Networking: LinkedIn and relevant communities
- Tracking: a spreadsheet, Notion, or Airtable that sits on top of the discovery sources

## Recruiting-layer noise and n-tier vendor chains
This is a different problem from ordinary job-board noise. In many cases, a company uses an MSP, VMS, or staffing vendor chain, and the opening gets passed through multiple recruiting layers before it reaches you.

### What this looks like
- A role is posted by a recruiting agency or vendor rather than the company directly
- Multiple agencies contact you about the same opening
- The role is re-badged, reworded, or farmed out to lower-tier vendors
- You see lots of outreach that looks related but is actually downstream noise

### Why it matters
This creates a lot of wasted effort because you may be applying to the same role through several different vendor layers instead of reaching the employer or direct recruiter.

### Tools and tactics that help
- Use a separate email alias or phone number for public boards and public applications
- Use tracking tools to deduplicate similar roles and identify the original employer
- Use Boolean search rules to exclude agency-heavy phrasing when searching on LinkedIn or other sources
- Use a simple blacklist of phrases such as:
  - "our client is looking for"
  - "W2 only"
  - "C2C"
  - "staffing"
  - "recruiting agency"
- Use direct company career pages and direct recruiter outreach when possible

### Practical stance
This is not a feature that standard job boards usually expose natively. The best defense is a combination of:
- tighter search filters
- better deduplication and tracking
- direct-source targeting
- selective use of automation that helps you filter noise rather than amplify it

## LinkedIn-specific filtering playbook
For your situation, LinkedIn should be used as a targeted sourcing channel rather than a broad network feed.

### Best use of LinkedIn
- Search exact role titles and technology combinations
- Filter to relevant industry, seniority, geography, and remote status
- Prioritize roles on actual company pages or employer-led posts
- Treat recruiter-only messages as low-priority unless they clearly match your fit threshold

### Screening questions to apply before you engage
- Does this role match at least 3 of these buckets: experience, stack, domain, quality of employer?
- Is the employer clear and direct?
- Does the description sound like a real product team or a vendor-heavy staffing chain?
- Does the role avoid obvious consulting, contractor, staffing, or offshore language?

### Recommended filters and exclusions
Use search filters and keyword exclusions for:
- recruiter
- agency
- staffing
- contractor
- consulting
- MSP
- VMS
- C2C
- W2
- vendor
- offshore
- subcontract

### Best practice
If a role does not clearly match your target profile and the employer is not obvious, skip it. Your time is better spent on a small number of high-signal roles than on a broad recruiter-heavy funnel.
