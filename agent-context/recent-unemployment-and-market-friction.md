# Recent Unemployment and Market Friction

## What the recent period felt like
- The recent unemployment period felt long, draining, and demoralizing.
- It was not just about being out of work; it was about being in a market that felt noisy, fragmented, and often performative.
- The experience made it clear that the problem was not only finding work, but finding work in a system that felt increasingly difficult to navigate.

## Main difficulties encountered
- The job market felt crowded with low-signal activity and recruiter noise.
- A lot of the process seemed driven by screening mechanics rather than genuine fit.
- Contracting work often felt unstable and uncertain, especially when the work was short-term or the company structure was unclear.
- Corporate hiring practices often felt opaque, overly procedural, and disconnected from actual engineering value.
- Recruiting noise made it hard to separate credible opportunities from generic or low-quality ones.

## Why this mattered
- The problem was not simply that the search took time.
- The problem was that the search often felt like it was happening inside systems that were more interested in process, optics, and volume than in clear fit or meaningful work.
- That made the experience exhausting and made it harder to trust the process.

## What this suggests about the next move
- The next move should aim for stability, clarity, and better signal.
- The goal is not to keep running through the same cycle of generic applications and recruiter churn.
- The goal is to target environments that are more coherent, more durable, and more aligned with actual strengths.

## Framing for future conversations
- The recent period can be described as a difficult but selective stretch in the market.
- It can be framed as a period of learning, filtering, and recalibrating rather than as a sign of weakness or drift.
- The point is to show that the search was deliberate and that the current strategy is more focused and more grounded.
