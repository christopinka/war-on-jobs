# Anti-Recruiter Noise Checklist

## Purpose
This is a short filtering checklist for reducing inbound spam from low-quality, third-tier recruiting vendors and staffing chains.

## Quick rules
- Do not reply to messages that do not name the employer clearly.
- Do not engage with agency-led roles unless the employer is obvious and the fit is strong.
- Do not respond to roles that are framed as contract, C2C, staffing, VMS, MSP, or vendor work unless you explicitly want that lane.
- Skip any role with vague company identity, weak domain clarity, or generic job wording.

## Red flags
- "Our client is looking for"
- staffing
- agency
- vendor
- MSP
- VMS
- C2C
- W2
- contract
- consulting
- offshore
- subcontract
- "resource"
- "bench"
- repeated versions of the same role across multiple agencies

## Screening questions
- Is the employer clearly named?
- Does the role match my experience and stack?
- Is the domain aligned with my interests?
- Is the company quality clear?
- Is the job communicated as an actual employer role or as a staffing chain?

## Decision rule
- If the employer is unclear, skip it.
- If the role has 2+ recruiter/vendor flags, skip it.
- If it is a repeated multi-agency posting, skip it.
- If it matches your target profile and the company is clear, continue.

## Practical workflow
1. Review only direct-employer or company-led roles.
2. Score the role on experience, stack, domain, and company quality.
3. Ignore any posting with staffing language or a vague employer.
4. Keep only the strongest matches in your tracker.

## Second-email rule
A second email is not a shield if you publish it broadly. If you put it on a resume or job site, it will be scraped and flooded quickly.

Use a job-search inbox only as a filtered relay:
- Keep it separate from your personal identity.
- Use different aliases for different channels, such as jobs, linkedin, and direct outreach.
- Route it through filters that auto-delete or archive vendor, staffing, MSP, and generic recruiter messages.
- Do not put a public-facing email on a resume unless you are prepared to filter and triage a large amount of noise.
- Prefer direct employer or LinkedIn contact paths over broadcasting a public inbox.

## Decision rule
- If the email is only for direct contacts, it is manageable.
- If it is on the resume or a public job board, expect spam immediately.
- If the volume exceeds your threshold, move to a stricter alias setup and stop using the public version.

## Goal
Reduce inbound calls and emails from lower-tier recruiter farms while preserving a small number of high-signal employer-led opportunities.
