# While-employed ongoing hire flow

## Purpose

This is the operating approach for a person who is still employed and has a finite runway before the end of their current contract or role, but does not want to rely on a vague “get back in touch later” model.

The objective is to create a continuous, low-noise hiring workflow that is active while employed, not only after the final notice period begins.

## Problem with the current model

Many hiring conversations end with:
- “let’s reconnect when you are closer to your end date”
- “we’ll revisit this once your contract is ending”
- “we’d like to stay in touch, but there is no active opening now”

This is a weak model for both sides.

It fails because:
- the hiring party has no operating plan for follow-through
- the candidate has no structured way to keep momentum alive
- the conversion or extension decision is left ambiguous
- the process starts too late, when urgency is already high

This creates a race condition where the candidate is forced into reactive scrambling rather than active pursuit.

## Design principle

The hiring process should be treated as an ongoing signal-chasing loop, not a single event.

A workable model is:
- maintain a live target-company and role queue
- keep the screening and review cadence regular while employed
- treat each role as a current opportunity, not a future opportunity
- maintain the candidate’s constraints honestly and clearly
- keep a consistent pipeline so that conversion and extension conversations can happen in parallel with active external pursuit

This avoids lying about availability and avoids the “we’ll reconnect later” trap.

## Working posture

The candidate should operate with the following honest stance:
- I am currently employed and want to finish my contract responsibly
- I am open to extension and conversion if the fit is strong
- I am also actively pursuing direct-employer and relevant opportunities in parallel
- I am not waiting until my end date to start this process
- I want a clear decision path, not vague future follow-up

This is honest, stable, and operationally useful.

## Operating model

### 1. Maintain an active target lane structure

Use target lanes for the actual market, not just a single broad list.

Primary lanes:
- core healthcare / healthcare software
- remote SaaS / global product companies
- healthcare-adjacent products
- consulting/recruiting watchlist as a secondary lane only

These lanes should stay narrow and relevant.

### 2. Build a role queue while employed

The role queue should be updated continuously with:
- company
- role title
- location
- source
- lane
- fit score
- direct employer or watchlist status
- application status
- follow-up date
- notes

This creates a reviewable backlog of real possibilities instead of a single reactive scramble.

### 3. Keep direct outreach honest and time-aware

The direct outreach message should not imply a false urgency or an immediate availability gap.

A stronger framing is:
- I am currently employed and want to complete my current commitment responsibly
- I am interested in a role that fits my background and long-term direction
- I would be open to discussing both current opportunities and timing should a future transition become relevant

This allows the employer to evaluate fit without forcing the candidate to lie about availability.

### 4. Separate three tracks

The ongoing pursuit should be organized into three separate tracks:

#### Track A: current employer relationship
- conversion conversation
- extension conversation
- internal mobility discussion
- timing and constraints clarity

#### Track B: direct-employer role pursuit
- healthcare / platform / cloud / backend / infrastructure roles
- remote and relevant geography targets
- applications to direct employers only

#### Track C: late-cycle signal management
- follow-up with recruiters or hiring managers who said to reconnect later
- staged re-engagement at the correct time
- no dependency on one contact to carry the whole process

These should not be merged into one vague pipeline.

## Decision rule

The right mindset is: keep the process active now, without pretending the timing issue does not exist.

The message to the market should be:
- I am engaged now
- I am evaluating opportunities deliberately
- I am open to strong match roles now and to future timing discussions as circumstances evolve

This reduces the brittleness of the “let’s revisit later” pattern.

## Recommended cadence

Use a weekly or biweekly operating rhythm:

- refresh target lanes
- review kept roles from the live screen
- add new direct-employer roles to the queue
- recheck high-priority company pages / LinkedIn company job pages
- follow up on outstanding opportunities
- record status and next action date

This keeps the motion continuous instead of reactive.

## Practical recommendations

### Keep a short, reviewable queue

Do not let the pipeline become a dumping ground for every vaguely relevant role.

A good working rule is:
- keep only roles that are a meaningful fit for your background, geography, and goals
- review the queue weekly and close out stale entries
- prefer a smaller set of high-signal roles over a large and noisy set
- if a role is not a real fit, do not keep it alive just because it is easy to apply to

This reduces the chance of performing “busy work” instead of actual hiring motion.

### Use direct messages only when the fit is real

Do not use direct outreach as a broad spray-and-pray activity. Use it only when:
- the role matches your target lane
- the company is a real strategic interest
- the company can plausibly hire someone in your profile
- the timing and fit are coherent enough to justify a message

If the fit is weak, the message becomes noise. If the fit is strong, it becomes leverage.

### Standardize the signal quality bar

Every conversation should answer four questions:
1. Is this a true fit for my background and career direction?
2. Is this company or team a meaningful target?
3. Is there a real hiring signal, not just exploratory chat?
4. Is there a clear next action or decision window?

If the answer is “no” to more than one of these, it should not be kept in the active queue.

### Set boundaries around timing honesty

The best framing is not “I’m unavailable” or “I’m looking for a job immediately.”

It is more effective to say:
- I am committed to my current role through my current period of employment
- I am evaluating high-fit opportunities deliberately
- I am open to timely conversations when there is a real match
- I prefer to be transparent about timing rather than create ambiguity

This keeps your credibility intact and prevents weak conversations from dragging on.

### Keep your current employer and your external search in separate tracks

The extensions, conversion, internal mobility conversations, and direct-employer search should not be conflated.

This is useful because:
- it prevents mixed messaging
- it keeps your search disciplined
- it avoids overcommitting to one lane while another lane is stronger
- it creates a clear record of what is active and what is not

### Treat the queue as an operating system, not a to-do list

A good role queue should answer:
- what is active now
- what is worth a follow-up
- what should be dropped
- who is the right contact
- what is the next decision point

This is more valuable than collecting a large number of vague opportunities.

## What to say to hiring teams

When a conversation reaches the “get back in touch later” point, the better framing is:

I am actively evaluating strong-fit opportunities while remaining committed to my current role through the end of my contract. I would prefer to have a clear conversation now about fit and timing so that if an opportunity is worthwhile, we can plan realistically rather than waiting until the last minute.

This is honest, stable, and operationally useful for both sides.

## Why this matters

The real challenge is not just landing an offer. It is creating a structured, ongoing search process that works while employed, especially when the candidate has a finite employment horizon.

That requires:
- active tracking
- honest timing communication
- direct-employer focus
- an ongoing role queue
- regular follow-up rather than one-off conversations

## Final principle

The candidate should not be forced into an all-or-nothing timing story.

The better approach is a continuously active pursuit model:
- keep the pipeline warm
- keep the timing honest
- keep the role list short and reviewable
- keep current employer, extension, and external options in parallel

This is the right model for a hiree with a finite runway and no appetite for vague follow-up.
